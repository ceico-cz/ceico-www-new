Mon 30 Nov 2026

String theoryMonday, 30 November 202614:00Room 226

One-loop self-energy of Type II heavy string states

Lorenzo Grimaldi Tor Vergata University of Rome, Italy

Abstract

The free string spectrum is highly degenerate, with a degeneracy that grows exponentially with the mass, making highly excited strings natural candidates for black-hole microstates in the string/black-hole correspondence. A non-vanishing string coupling $g_s$ makes massive states unstable and shifts their masses, partially lifting this degeneracy. At one loop, the imaginary part of the self-energy gives the tree-level width for the decay into two lower-mass states, whereas the real part gives the mass shift and is generally IR-divergent. We firstly focus on the states of maximal spin at each level in the NS-NS sector of Type II theories, for which the analysis simplifies because they cannot mix with other states. Exploiting elliptic functions, we obtain a closed-form expression for the worldsheet integral on the torus. We then regularize and renormalize the remaining modular integral via the $i\varepsilon$-prescription, extracting mass shifts and decay widths up to level $N=10$.

String theoryMonday, 11 January 202714:00Room 226

Towards the explicit construction of generic 2d CFTs

António Antunes LPENS, Paris, France

Abstract

Our understanding of interacting CFTs in d>2 dimensions was revolutionized by modern developments in the analytical and numerical conformal bootstrap. The poster child of this program, the non-exactly solvable 3d Ising CFT, is now understood to an unprecedented level of detail. In contrast, our understanding of 2d CFTs encompasses large classes of exactly solvable models (the so-called rational CFTs), and a set of universal bootstrap results which apply only to "generic" non-exactly solvable CFTs of which no concrete example is available. In this talk, we summarize our recent attempts at constructing such theories by coupling exactly solvable CFTs and flowing to IR fixed points. We will discuss perturbative and non-perturbative constructions as well as some dual bounds on the set of conserved currents in the IR..

25 Nov

Cosmology11:30Institute of Plasma Physics building - room 421

Cosmological gravitational particle production and implications for dark matter phenomenology

Duarte Feiteira University of Helsinki, Finland

Abstract

Particle production in the Early Universe is one of the cornerstones of modern cosmology and plays an important role in dark matter phenomenology. In this talk, I will first discuss the gravitational production of scalars during inflation. I will compare Bogolyubov coefficient and Starobinsky stochastic approaches, showing that they only agree in the limit of infinitely long inflation. High-scale inflation is very efficient in particle production, leading to constraints on the existence of free, light, and stable scalars: such particles are viable only if their masses are below the eV scale or if the reheating temperature is in the GeV range. These constraints motivate scenarios in which dark matter is produced via low-temperature freeze-in. In this framework, I will consider warm Higgs portal dark matter. In scenarios where the Standard Model thermal bath never reached high temperatures, dark matter production can be suppressed. This allows for a significant dark matter-Higgs coupling, enabling warm dark matter detection via Higgs decay at colliders. I will further discuss the strong Lyman-α bound on the dark matter mass, excluding masses below 50-100 keV, and show how the shape of the warm dark matter momentum distribution is highly non-thermal and not captured by the commonly used αβγ-parametrization.

18 Nov

Cosmology11:30A. Kochanovské Hall

Standard Model Effective Field Theory and Oscillons (and vortices)

Andrzej Wereszczyński Jagiellonian University, Kraków, Poland

Abstract

I will show that the inclusion of a dimension-six operator in the Higgs potential has a dramatic impact on the stability of oscillons in the SU (2) bosonic sector of the Standard Model, extending their lifetime by more than an order of magnitude. This happens for the physical value of the ratio between the Higgs and W boson masses, m_H /m_W =1.556 and for the dimension-six operator O_6 = (Φ†Φ)^3 whose coupling constant is below the current upper bound. I will also show how this operator changes the formation of vortices, which form the ground state of EW theory at a large external magnetic field.
